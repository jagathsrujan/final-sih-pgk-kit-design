Exaggerated dimensions
Declared scale is 1 unit = 10 mm, and the axial stations and radial build follow 03-caa-machine-sizing.md. Three things are drawn out of scale for visibility, and each is labelled on screen:
• Air gap — drawn 5.5 mm (0.55 unit). Design value 0.40 mm (C35), so this is roughly 14× exaggerated.
• Bearing balls — ball diameter drawn 5.6 mm. The races are at a realistic mean Ø94 mm (the design ring OD is ≈Ø98 mm), so only the ball size is enlarged.
• Canard span — tips drawn at Ø166 mm. The design caps canard tips at Ø151 mm (C31) so they stay inside the Ø155 mm bore when stowed. Span is drawn long so the two pairs read clearly.
Everything else — body Ø15.49 units, wall thicknesses, station spacing, 12 slots / 14 poles, and the 1.00 unit clearance between the bearing outer race and the body hub — is to scale.

The two parallel interfaces

The bearing and the PMSM are PARALLEL interfaces between the same two bodies.

BEARING → carries 100 % of structural loads PMSM → carries 100 % of torque control

The bearing keeps the air gap alive.
The PMSM controls the roll angle.

Neither can replace the other.

How to read this visualization

1. The bearing is the only thing holding the nose on. Its inner race is seated on the shell-body hub, its outer race is pressed into the canard-assembly bore. Every newton of the 73.5 kN setback, all bending and all radial load goes through those balls — and through nothing else. 2. The PMSM touches nothing. Rotor magnets are mounted on the nose, stator windings on the body, and a 0.40 mm air gap sits between them. It can only ever exchange torque. It is physically incapable of carrying a structural load, and the air gap is only alive because the bearing holds position across it. 3. Together they give a body spinning at 250 rev/s a nose that holds a commanded roll angle — so the fixed canards can point somewhere useful. The bearing makes it hold; the motor makes it aim. Remove either one and the concept fails.

One honest consequence worth watching in the readouts: in every controlled state the electromagnetic torque opposes the relative rotation, so the machine is absorbing — never idling. Holding the ring against full body spin is a kilowatt-class duty that has to be dissipated (register items W7 / W12).

Technical accuracy self-check

Where this picture and the register differ

Everything below is a deliberate difference between the drawing and the criteria register, not a defect — but it is listed so a reader who checks one against the other is not left guessing. The register is authoritative in every case. Recorded 2026-09-19.

Canard azimuths. The two pairs are drawn at −45° / +135° (steering) and +45° / +225° (differential), purely so the cutaway reads clearly. The register's convention is 0° / 180° steering and 90° / −90° control (C1). Same architecture, rotated for framing. Torque reference for the power readouts — resolved 2026-09-23. The readouts multiply the duty torque by the launch spin, 1 623 rad/s. Holding therefore shows ≈3.7 kW, and that figure is an artefact, not the load: it pairs a duty torque with the launch spin, where the flight's maximum torque (2.119 N·m) occurs on descent at t ≈ 86.5 s. The load is the pointwise product, which peaks at 2.92 kW at t = 0 — so C42's 2.92 kW stands and is the number that governs W7/W12 and P6. Register risk 46 is closed on this resolution. Do not quote the on-screen figure as C42's value. Exaggerated dimensions. Air gap drawn ≈14× (design 0.40 mm, C35 — the tooltip's "0.5–1 mm" is the drawn width, not the specification); canard tips drawn at Ø166 mm against the Ø151 mm design cap (C31), i.e. the exaggerated tip radius the code itself labels. Bus voltage is not shown. The readouts say "→ bus" and "→ dump" without a number on purpose: H8 set the bus at ≈150–200 V (180 V nominal), below the flight-minimum EMF, superseding the 400 V of the 2026-09-18 sizing pass. Any figure this file shows from that pass is historical.
