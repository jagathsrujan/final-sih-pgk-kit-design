DRISHTI-PGK — the system architecture, detailed

Four subsystems inside a fuze-sized volume — canard actuation, guidance, navigation and control, the multi-mode fuze, and power and the pre-fire interface — drawn as the three chains that carry the round's behaviour: what flies and commands, what generates and sheds, and what arms and functions. Every value on the sheet carries the criterion that owns it; the register is the authority for all of them.

The architecture — three chains and the shell they serve

an interface in our design gated, one-way, or not yet ruled an open or unruled value amber text: a value nothing may claim yet one hop, at the single crossing: every other line routes in a corridor

Chain order — flight: pre-launch set-up → navigation & sensing → guidance & control → canard ring · power: alternator → power conditioning → bus, dump and buffer → loads and actuator drive · fuze: sensor suite → central controller → safe & arm and output → detonator. The amber path is the flight's heat leaving through the fuze well, and it is the architecture's price.

How the three chains meet

One machine is both the actuator and the generator. The magnets ride the ring and the coils sit on the body, so drawing current puts a torque on the ring and not drawing it puts power on the bus. That is why the sheet has no servo, no gearbox and no flight battery: the actuator is the alternator, and the same winding that steers the round is the one that supplies it [C34, C14, C19]. It is also the architecture's cost, and the sheet states it rather than hiding it: 2.92 kW peak and 121 kJ per flight must be shed, against a servo kit that carries no such load at all [C42].

The ring angle is the only command channel. The magnitude of the guidance force is set by cant angle and dynamic pressure and cannot be switched off, so the law may only aim it [G2]. A partial correction is therefore a dwell-fraction phasor: hold the aim azimuth for the fraction of the cycle the authority allows, and park at aim ±90° for the rest. The authority gate is a computed condition rather than a schedule, which is why its edges are drawn dashed: the mechanism is ours, the 15 s and 35–55 s edges come from the reference force profile and stay provisional until the aero set exists [G3, risk 8].

The fuze is separately powered, separately armed, and dead by default. It keeps its own MCU on its own rail, in the reserve battery domain, so it arms and functions with the kit's computer silent [H10, W5, H12]. The kit crosses that boundary twice, both times one-way and bounded: the in-flight time re-synchronisation the airburst needs, and the predicted-miss veto, which is nothing more than the withholding of the third arming condition [F1, F16, F6]. A guidance-side fault degrades the navigation; it cannot reach the firing train.

Everything the kit generates ends in the shell. 121 kJ cannot be stored (a 1.5 F bank at the bus would neither fit nor survive launch) and only about a quarter can be rejected to the airstream, so the design closes by conduction through the fuze well into 45 kg of steel: a 5.8 K rise and a requirement of ≥58 W/K at the peak [W7, W12, P6]. The bare 2″-12 UNS thread delivers 3–16 W/K, so the closing route is the well bore as a contacting radial path plus a conductance-specified joint, and it is verified on the rig rather than assumed [P6, R7]. That requirement, and not the electronics, is what shapes the joint, the dump's location and the load path.

What the detailed sheet changes from the envelope block diagram

The architecture is the same; four statements are corrected, because the register does not support the envelope wording.

1. “Supercapacitor: high-capacity storage” is a power buffer, not storage. 121 kJ at the bus needs ≈1.5 F, which neither fits the envelope nor survives 15 000 g, so the design does not store the flight's energy at all — it conducts it into the shell. The 150–200 J bank exists for the fireset reservoir, hold-up and motoring bursts [W4, W7].

2. “During apogee, powers actuator for canard control” inverts the arithmetic. Generation floors at 0.24 kW at apogee against ≈12 W of demand: ≈20:1, the whole flight. The design question is where the surplus goes, never whether there is enough [W4, C42].

3. “System loads: GNC + sensors + fuze + electronics” is one rail domain too few. The fuze keeps its own MCU and its own always-alive rail, in the reserve-battery domain, so that a fault in the guidance domain cannot reach the arming path; the two domains hand over through a passive diode-OR [H10, H12, W5].

4. “Alternator” and “actuator drive” are the same machine seen twice. The block diagram puts the two in different tiers; physically the magnets are on the ring and the coils on the body, so the drive current is the torque and the un-drawn current is the supply [C34, C9, C19].

Two things the envelope diagram carries that this sheet keeps deliberately: the three-descent shape (pre-launch set-up → flight → the terminal chain) and the fuze at the end of its own chain. What it adds is the wiring: the reference sheet shows no connector between the power system and the loads, and none into the fuze. Those interfaces are the interesting part of this design.

Declared open — and what closes each item

Nothing amber on this sheet is a claim. These are the items drawn open, in the order the design meets them, with the thing that closes each one. All of the rig-verified items are gated on shortlisting and are presented in the submission as a plan, never as an asset [R7, P7].

  • Ring friction at flight speed [C13, C25] — the inherited 0.10 N·m was measured at 83.8 rad/s on a rig, 19.4× below the flight's relative speed. Closed by a load-dominated bearing selection and by the rig at ≈2 000 rad/s.
  • The fuze-well thermal joint [P6] — 3–16 W/K today against a ≥58 W/K peak. Closed by the well bore as a contacting radial path, a conductance-specified joint (which makes the torque spec a thermal requirement), and a rig measurement in W/K.
  • Arming thresholds and distance [F7, F8] — the stimuli are named (15 000 g, 176–304 Hz); the thresholds and the arming distance are not yet set, and G3's opening edge rides on them.
  • The authority gate's edges [G3, risk 8] — the mechanism is ours, the 15 s and 35–55 s edges are the reference force profile. Closed when the aerodynamic coefficient set exists.
  • The swerve-response phase [G16, risk 51] — a control force ahead of the CG swerves the round at a phase off the force direction (published: ≈172° at launch to 146–149° near apogee, on another airframe). Method transfers, the number does not; G4 allocates 9 m to ≈1.3° of direction error, so this line is the ring-angle budget's largest open item.
  • Canard geometry and the Q floor [C4, C5, C31] — 80 mm chord, 26.5 mm exposed span at the Ø151 mm tip cap; the values are stated, the rows are still OPEN.
  • Ring-side mass, and with it the clock on three rows [P9, C26, C32, T4–T6] — the CAD weighs the kit at 2.713 kg against a ≤1.4 kg limit; the ring carrier is the lever that pays mass, setback load and the junction step at once.
