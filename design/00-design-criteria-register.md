# DRISHTI-PGK — Design Criteria Register

**Problem statement:** SIH26098 — *Development of a Low-Cost Precision Guidance and Smart Electronic Fuze System for a 155 mm Artillery Shell*
**Organisation:** Ministry of Defence (MoD) / Yantra India Limited, Ambajhari, Nagpur
**Purpose of this file:** the single working record of every criterion the design must satisfy or decide. It is the evidence base for the submission — every number that appears in the submission should trace back to a row here.

---

## How to use this file

**Every criterion carries one of three types:**

| Type | Meaning | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Given** | Imposed by the customer, the platform, or a defence standard | Extract it. Do not invent it. |
| **Derived** | Falls out of physics once the givens are fixed | Compute it. Show the arithmetic and the assumptions. |
| **Chosen** | A design decision of ours | Decide it, and justify it against the givens with a trade. |

**Status values — keep them honest:**

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `OPEN` | Not yet defined |
| `OPEN-BLOCKED` | *(retired 2026-09-17 — superseded by `PARAMETRIC`)* Cannot be fixed until external data arrives. |
| `PARAMETRIC` | **Sized over a declared envelope built from public data** (`design/02-reference-155mm-model.md`). Neither blocked nor parked: a bounded design value is in use, with a stated public basis and a sensitivity showing how the design closes across the envelope. Every PARAMETRIC value is re-baselined at P6. |
| `PROPOSED` | A value is proposed but not yet confirmed by the team |
| `DEFINED` | Confirmed by the team |
| `DERIVED` | Computed from other defined criteria |
| `VERIFIED` | Confirmed by analysis, simulation or test |

**Governing criteria are marked ⚑.** These are where the margins are thinnest and where the design will be won or lost. Get these right before anything else.

**Values marked *provisional* are derived on top of a placeholder, an unconfirmed anchor, or a [ref] value.** A provisional value names the input it depends on and never counts as closed, however solid the arithmetic looks.

**Values marked **[ref]** are carried from the reference literature for the 155 mm class (`notes/00-PGK-thesis-working-notes.md`) and must be confirmed against YIL's actual shell.**

## The dependency chain

Criteria cannot be defined out of order. This is the sequence:

```
M  Mission (target, range, charge)
     └─ baseline unguided dispersion
         └─ required correction capability
             └─ G  guidance & control requirements
                 └─ C  canard sizing → Taero → alternator sizing → friction budget
                     └─ N  navigation & sensing requirements
                         └─ W  power, thermal
                             └─ S/P/E/F  structure, stability, environment, fuze
                                 └─ K/R  cost, verification
```

S  (Stability) and P (Platform) run in parallel with the chain — they constrain it, and they are gated on the YIL shell data.

That is a change to the **employment concept**, not just to the kit, and it is the kind of change that turns a product into a system. **Mitigation:** X14 must state the dependency and who provides it, X15 must state the weather limitation honestly — laser does not penetrate cloud, GNSS does — and the module must not be described anywhere as a drop-in capability | X2, X14, X15, R4 | Open — unchanged by X1; X2/X14/X15 still owe the designator dependency and the weather limitation, and the module may not be described anywhere as a drop-in capability |
| 32 | **The de-spun architecture carries a kilowatt-class energy load that the reference work never measured, and no criterion currently owns it.** Because the machine is a *loaded generator*, holding the ring costs `P = Taero × ω_body` — **2.92 kW at launch, ≈121 kJ over the flight** (C42). The reference alternator is rated **1.2 kW continuous, exceeded ×2.4**, and the bench rig ran body spin **21.6× slower**, so the same torque produced 21.6× less power and the problem was invisible. **This is the second instance of the rig's speed scaling hiding a full-scale failure mode** (the first being the apogee control loss). The energy cannot be stored — 121 kJ would need ≈1.5 F at 400 V, which will not fit the envelope or survive 15 000 g — so it must be dissipated as heat, in a nose that is already aerodynamically heated (W9, W10) and already over-subscribed for volume (risk 27). **Mitigation:** W7 must be sized against C42 rather than treated as a detail; the dump's mass, surface area and location must be resolved with the nose-packing budget; and the trade against a **servo-actuated canard architecture, which has no such load at all**, must be documented rather than assumed away | C42, C20, C43, W7, W9, W10, W1, risk 27, risk 24 | Open — new; **architectural, not a component issue** |
| 33 | **The machine's mass is the dominant item in the kit and pushes the retrofit past the published benchmark.** Sizing gives **≈0.81–0.97 kg** for the machine alone (C40), against the fielded M1156's **1.4 kg for the entire kit** — and the M1156 achieves that with no alternator at all. The net penalty over the replaced ≈0.98 kg fuze moves from the reference model's **+0.42 kg (0.9 %)** toward **+0.7–0.9 kg**, with the CG shift moving from 2.6 mm to roughly **6–8 mm forward**. **Mitigation:** the shift is still only ≈0.8 % of projectile length and sits inside the platform's own 0.55–0.62 L envelope spread, so it is a soft failure, not a hard one. But S1 must check the **direction** of the stability effect — mass forward reduces the CP–CG separation, which reduces `C_Mα` and should *raise* `Sg`, the opposite of the usual intuition.
