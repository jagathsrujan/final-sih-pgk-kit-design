# Market pictures — sources and licences

Pictures used on the fielded-system pages of `design/09-professor-briefing.html`
(pages 3–6, credited again on page 44).

Two are **public-domain works of the US Government** or a public-domain
briefing graphic; one comes from an **open-access journal paper under CC BY
3.0**. Nothing here is a manufacturer's marketing image, and nothing has been
altered beyond cropping and scaling.

| File | Figure | What it shows | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `m1156-diagram.jpg` | 2 | XM1156 section alongside a standard fuze profile | P. J. Burke, A. Pergolizzi — NDIA Fuze Conference briefing, 2010 | Public domain |
| `wang-correction-part.jpg` | 3 | (a) A spin-stabilised projectile with fixed canards and (b) the correction part down the axis: steering canards, rotation canards, roll-angle convention | Wang, Song, Fang and Guo, *International Journal of Aerospace Engineering*, 2015, Article 908304, Figures 3 and 1 | CC BY 3.0 (open access) |
| `excalibur-m982.jpg` | 26 | M982 Excalibur, the purpose-built guided projectile alternative | US Army | Public domain |

The two panels of Figure 3 were cropped from a 300 dpi render of the published
PDF and composed into one picture; nothing inside the panels was altered.

## Held for reference, not embedded

- `correction-part-round.jpg` — panel (a) alone, the projectile carrying a
  correction fuze. Kept because it is a usable single-panel version if the deck
  ever needs the round on its own.
- **A watermarked stock photograph** of a fuze setter at work in Afghanistan was
  offered and cannot be reproduced. The scene is a US Army photograph, so a
  public-domain original would be usable; the deck uses the reference thesis's
  own hardware and rig photographs instead (see `../thesis/README.md`).

## Superseded

`canard-principle.jpg` — a sketch of the correction part supplied by the team.
Its original source could not be established, so it was replaced by the same
idea from the published paper (Figure 3 above), whose authorship and licence
can be stated. The distinction matters: a figure that cannot be credited must
not sit in a document that credits everything else.
